# UK University Admissions Equity Analysis
Capstone project for the Imperial College London Professional Certificate in Data Analytics. Examines socioeconomic inequality in UK university admissions and whether disadvantage at entry compounds into early-career graduate earnings gaps, using UCAS and DfE LEO data.

Socioeconomic Diversity in UK University Admissions: Does Disadvantage at Entry Compound into Lower Early-Stage Graduate Career Earnings?

Imperial College London — Professional Certificate in Data Analytics | Capstone Project | Anatoly Reeves | September 2026

Overview

This project examines socioeconomic inequality in UK higher education — specifically, whether disadvantage at the point of university entry compounds into disadvantage at the point of graduate employment.

Using publicly available UCAS admissions data and Department for Education Longitudinal Educational Outcomes (LEO) data, the project analyses acceptance patterns across 21 broad subject areas (CAH1) over six admissions cycles (2019–2024), linked to median graduate earnings at 3 Years After Graduation (3YAG).

Findings are cross-validated across two independent deprivation measures — POLAR4 and the pooled national deprivation indices (IMD2019, NIMDM2017, SIMD2020, WIMD2019) — yielding a Pearson correlation of r = 0.85.

Key Findings
Medicine and Dentistry has a Q5:Q1 acceptance ratio of 4.7, meaning students from the most advantaged areas are nearly five times more likely to be accepted than those from the most disadvantaged — and it is also the highest-earning subject at £47,212 median 3YAG

Mathematical Sciences shows a similar pattern, representing the clearest cases of compounding disadvantage in the dataset

Subjects Allied to Medicine is the most equitable subject by both measures, with above-median earnings — demonstrating that high equity and strong graduate outcomes are not mutually exclusive

Across most subjects, equity gaps have remained broadly stable over 2019–2024, suggesting structural barriers are not improving

Access equity does not guarantee financial equity — the largest cluster of subjects are relatively accessible but deliver below-median earnings
